title: Hazell, Watson & Viney dropcaps

A collection of historical dropcaps and illuminated initials restored from scans of volumes printed by [*Hazell, Watson & Viney*](https://www.aylesburyremembered.com/Businesses/Industry/Hazells) between 1898 and 1902.<br>Restoration technique inspired by [Century Library](https://centurylibrary.com/).

## On information and belief
These dropped initials are a set of decorative woodcut letters believed to be produced by Hazell, Watson & Viney (or one of their apprentices)[^apprentice] in the UK between approximately 1895 and 1905, likely at their Tring Road works in Aylesbury, Buckinghamshire.

They are characterised by bold, black rectangular blocks with large serif letters surrounded by intricate botanical motifs. It is not yet known if a full 26-letter set was ever made.

> [!info] We're missing some initials...
> Can you help complete the collection? We currently have 13/26 of the initials restored and are in search of the remaining initials. If you have any reference material or high-resolution scans of these dropcaps or have any information you think may help us find them, please [[contact|contact me]]!

## Known occurences
The surviving dropcaps in this collection have been identified and digitally restored from the following late Victorian and Edwardian publications.

1. *[Chronicles and Stories of Old Bingley](https://archive.org/details/chroniclesstorie00speiiala)* (Harry Speight)<br>— Published in 1898 by Elliot Stock; printed in London.
1. *[Antonio Stradivari, His Life and Work (1644–1737)](https://archive.org/details/cu31924022328896)* (W. E., Arthur F. & Alfred E. Hill)<br>— Published in 1902 by W. E. Hill & Sons; printed at Aylesbury.

[^apprentice]: It is believed that the letters **OCG** in the centers of the Rose blossoms of the **D** initial are the initials of the apprentice who carved the original woodblock designs, although this has not been corroborated. So far, no-one by the initials OCG has been identified as having worked with, or for, Hazell, Watson & Viney.
[^specimen]: The primary master proof sheet for this set is believed to be preserved in this item. This would, at a minimum, allow us to see how many letters were created.
[^naming]: I assume each initial is decorated with a flower whose name starts with the letter of the initial. That said, I am not a botanist and have likely misidentified some of the flowers. If you think I've made a mistake, please let me know!
